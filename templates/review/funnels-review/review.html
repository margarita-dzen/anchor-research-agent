Shelly funnels: review and sign off

Everything built so far, in the order it happens: the ads, the pages they land on, and the order flow. Approve each one, or ask for a change and write what should change. Your answers save as you go, so you can stop and come back. 0 of 0 reviewed.

Please read first

What changed since our call on 16 September

So nothing here reads as a surprise. The last group is the part we cannot finish without you.

What we agreed on the call

  • Bundle prices now sit inside the product instead of a discount at checkout. The full list is 42 unlisted products, six of which you already have.
  • Two and three kits are priced as their own products, so no rule has to work out a quantity price.

The order flow

  • Adding to the order is a visual confirmation now, not a cart page.
  • The pages after it carry the proof: before and after, customer photos, the listing, the device numbers and the smart-home systems you work with.
  • "Check availability and price" shows availability and the price, read live from your store, instead of putting the kit straight in the cart.
  • On the articles that is a text link at 45 percent of the page, not a sticky add-to-cart bar.
  • Fixed: taking the first upsell jumped to checkout and skipped the second offer. The offer timers now reset with every new order.
  • The product page gained a customer quote under the bundle name, the saving, how far the order is from free delivery, frequently bought together, the works-with logos, reviews with real buyer photos and a gallery you can swipe.
  • The 1-2-3 tier cards are gone. There is a plus and a minus instead.
  • Declining the first upsell is one clear button.
  • The three-for-two and the buy-one-get-one now show a picture of the deal.

The articles, after a compliance pass

  • Every page that is not Shelly's own now says SPONSORED in the masthead, above the headline. It used to appear only in the footer, below the buy button.
  • The three personal stories are written in a first person who is not a real customer. They now carry "Advertisement" in the masthead and a footer note saying the narrator is not a real person, and we would rather you decide whether to keep them, rebuild them around a real, consenting customer, or drop them.
  • An internal navigation bar naming the three funnels was visible to anyone who opened a page without an ad link. It is out of the published pages.
  • Three headlines promised something the article then contradicted. "Twelve things" became six, "two of them cost nothing" became three cost almost nothing, and "stop a flood" became catch a leak, because the kit reports water, it does not close it.
  • The energy savings were worked out over a full day when the schedule only switches things off for seven hours. They are recalculated and the sum is printed on the page.
  • The review blocks say four stars and below are left in, so they now always include the lowest-scored review.

Waiting on you before anything can run

  • The Smart Security Bundle sells at 87.80 on your store and our offer is 89.90, so the page shows a saving below zero. Either we go under 87.80 or you tell us what is really in it.
  • The crossed-out prices are what the parts cost separately, not a previous price. We propose writing "64.60 bought separately" instead of a strike-through.
  • The flood kit's humidity sensor needs a gateway, and there is no gateway in the box. Add one and reprice, or we say so on every page.
  • The company figures, the SDAX line and the CES award need a yes from investor relations and a date they are true as of.
  • One source and one date for the review score, so the same number appears everywhere.
