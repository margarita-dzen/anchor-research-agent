# Angles report: template and rules

Claude writes this report from the digest files (`digest.txt`, optional `competitors-digest.txt`, `social-digest.txt`, `comments-digest.txt`). It answers one question: **which angles should we run, and how, based on what is already proven to sell?**

Save it as `<project>-angles.md` in the research folder. Write it in English first (precise, quotable). The team-language version comes in the deliverable phase.

## Rules for every report

- **Every claim points to evidence:** advertiser, country, days running, and ad id. Example: `(BrandName DE, 412d, facebook_123456789)`. No id, no claim.
- **Hooks are verbatim** in the original language, then `Gloss:` with the English meaning. Remove emojis; replace long dashes with a plain hyphen.
- **Competitor claims are theirs, not facts.** Say "they claim". Never copy a medical claim, a study, a number or a review into our copy.
- **Say what the data cannot show** (section 8). A missing market or a noisy keyword is a finding, not something to hide.
- Plain language, short sentences, no emojis, no em-dashes. No "not X, it is Y", no slogan endings.
- Ranking logic: long-running proof in big foreign markets first, then the size of the gap in our markets, then product fit.

## Skeleton

```markdown
# <Brand or product>: angles to scale (<markets>)

**Direction used:** <e.g. find what is proven in DE/FR/UK, rework it for BG and RO>
**Sources:** <digest files, number of ad rows, unique ads, which languages and markets, date of the run>
**How to read this:** days running is the main proof an ad makes money; reach and spend are estimates. Hooks are verbatim; "Gloss" = English meaning. All competitor claims are theirs.

---

## 0. The short version
5 to 7 bullets. What is proven abroad and empty at home, the single biggest opportunity, the format that travels, the offer pattern that keeps coming back, and the main risk.

## 1. What was kept and what was dropped
| Group | Advertisers | Why dropped |
Keyword noise (wrong meaning of the search word), off-category, adjacent audiences. Note any dropped ad that still proves a FORMAT (for example long first-person story ads in another niche running 300+ days).

## 2. Angle map
| # | Angle | Core promise (one line) | Proven where | Best proof (days, advertiser, ad id) | Status in market 1 | Status in market 2 | Product fit |
One row per angle. Status = busy / open / only competitor type X.

## 3. Angle details
One block per angle:
- **Framing:** how the problem is described.
- **Outcome:** what the customer gets, in their words.
- **Mechanism / reason-why:** why the usual fix fails and why this one works.
- **Proof type:** demo, expert, UGC, story, comparison, numbers, press.
- **Hooks:** 3 to 6 verbatim hooks with Gloss and (advertiser country, days, reach, ad id).
- **Formats seen:** video demo, talking head, static image, carousel, advertorial, quiz.
- **Local status:** who runs it in our markets, if anyone.
- **Claim caution:** what we must not say, and the safe version.

## 4. Top angles ranked by expected upside
| Rank | Angle | Proven where | Strongest proof abroad | Gap at home | Product |
Then 2 to 3 lines on why the order is what it is.

## 5. Creative concepts and advertorial outlines for the top 3 to 4
Per angle:
- **Advertorial outline:** headline (team language + Gloss), 6 to 8 beats, CTA as a text link.
- **Creative concepts table:** | # | Hook text | Visual | Format |
Mark every testimonial, expert quote and result as [real, to be sourced] until it exists.

## 6. Offer patterns that keep coming back
| Pattern | Examples (verbatim, days, ad id) | How we could use it |
Typical rows: buy X get a gift, 2-pack default, 1+1, bundle as a set, money-back guarantee, warranty, expert quote, price anchor vs the professional alternative, delivery speed, review counts, subscription/refills, cash on delivery.

## 7. Objections and customer words (if comments were pulled)
- Top objections ranked by how often they appear, each with 1 to 2 verbatim comments + Gloss + source.
- Words customers use for the problem and the outcome (use these in hooks).
- Questions that show the awareness level (do they ask "what is it" or "how do I do it right").

## 8. What to avoid
- Hooks or claims Meta rejects or that break our copy rules (medical claims, body shaming, invented studies, fake scarcity, fake reviews, before/after without proof).
- Angles that look busy but are all one big brand with a budget we cannot match.
- Formats that only work with something we do not have (a real doctor, real before/after photos).

## 9. Rolling out to other languages
Per extra market: which angles first, any local twist, which searches are still missing.

## 10. Gaps in this data
Thin markets, zero-result searches, noisy keywords, ads without copy, anything not verified (for example creatives not viewed, only copy read).

## Appendix: key ads by age
| Days | Advertiser | Country | Format | Hook (first line) | Landing page | Ad id |
Top 20 to 30, most proven first.
```

## Quality check before you hand it over

- [ ] Every hook has advertiser, days and ad id.
- [ ] Every non-English hook has a Gloss.
- [ ] No competitor claim is presented as a fact.
- [ ] The ranking explains why.
- [ ] Section 10 lists real gaps.
- [ ] No emojis, no em-dashes, no "not X, it is Y".
- [ ] No client-private information, no Anchor branding if a client will read it.
